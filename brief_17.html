2️⃣ Review & Real-World Connection

Static Duration
00:05–00:13
Personalization Trigger #2 of 5 — adapt pace live Watch how fast the student answers the ×2 recall (Case Study 4: "2 × 6"). If they answer in under 5 seconds without hesitation → skip slowing down, move briskly through the remaining review cases. If they hesitate or answer wrong → stop, show 2 fingers × 6 groups on your hand/screen, re-count together out loud before letting them retry.
Required interactive review (≥2 questions, respond actively)
  • Case Study 1 (repeated addition, MCQ) — after they answer, ask the open follow-up: "Why is 4+4+4 the right one and not 3+3+3+3?" Listen for them explaining "groups of 4" — this is your specific understanding check.
  • Case Study 2 (True/False, inverse operations) — after they answer, ask: "If I told you 6×5=30, what division fact could you make instantly?" (expect 30÷5=6 or 30÷6=5). This previews the concept coming in Stage 3.
Walk through all 10 review cases together

Let the student answer every case (mix of MCQ, True/False, fill-in, sorting, pairing, checkbox, drag-and-drop) themselves by clicking/dragging — you narrate and confirm, you do not click for them. Read every explanation box out loud after each submit, even correct ones, to reinforce the reasoning.

🎯 Goal by 00:13: student is confident that multiplication = repeated addition, multiplication/division are opposites, and the ×2 pattern is fresh in memory. Deviation budget for this stage: ≤1 minute.
Bridging line into Stage 3"You just proved you're a multiplication expert with the number 2 — now let's discover the secret pattern of a brand new number: 5!"

3️⃣ Concept Exploration: Table of 5

Static Duration
00:13–00:28
Personalization Trigger #3 of 5 — pick ONE real-world hook The material shows 3 hooks (hand/fingers, star, clock). Before class, based on this student's interest, decide which one to emphasize and linger on; move quickly past the other two. Note your choice here each session: ______________________
Thinking-aloud script while showing the skip-count track
Say while pointing at each number appearing "Watch my finger — 5... plus 5 more is...10... plus 5 more is... 15. Do you notice something about the last digit of every number? [pause for student] That's right — it's always 0 or 5! That's our secret pattern."
🔴 Deliberate mistake demonstration (required — do not skip)
Exact script — the mistake is scripted, not accidental-sounding Problem to use: 8 × 5. Skip-count out loud while raising a finger each time, and at the 6th jump, deliberately say the wrong number:
"5... 10... 15... 20... 25... 31?? Hmm, wait — that doesn't look right, let me recount from the start." (Recount: 5,10,15,20,25,30,35,40.) "Ah I see my mistake — I jumped too far. It's 30, not 31, because we're always adding exactly 5. See how easy it is to slip up? That's why we always double-check by recounting."

Purpose: models metacognition and shows the student that mistakes are normal and checkable — do not rush past this, let the student see you visibly "catch" it.
Misconception to explicitly correct
Some students think ×5 answers only end in "5". Explicitly say: "Notice: 2×5=10 ends in 0, but 3×5=15 ends in 5 — it alternates between 0 and 5 every step. Both are correct patterns of the same table."
Step-by-step button (6 × 5)

When you reach the numbered Step 1–4 buttons in the material, click them one at a time — narrate each step in your own words before revealing the next, do not click through quickly. Ask "What do you think Step 3 will show?" before clicking Step 3, to keep the student predicting.

10 case studies

Same approach as Stage 2 — student clicks/drags, you narrate, read every explanation aloud. This stage carries the heaviest new content, budget the most time here.

🎯 Goal by 00:28: student can recite the skip-count sequence 5→50 unaided and states the 0/5 pattern rule in their own words. Deviation budget: ≤1 minute.
Bridging line into Stage 4"Now that you know the pattern, let's put on our thinking caps and solve some real puzzles with it."

4️⃣ Guided Practice: Think It Through

Static Duration
00:28–00:40
Personalization Trigger #4 of 5 — scaffold live If the student answers Case Studies 1–3 correctly on the first try, skip ahead and let them work with less narration for the rest of this stage. If they miss any of the first 3, slow down and use the hint ladder below before every remaining question.
Open-ended questions to ask out loud (≥5 required — tick as you use them)
  • "How do you know if a number belongs in the 5 times table, just by looking at it?"
  • "What pattern do you notice in the last digit of every answer?"
  • "Can you explain why 30 ÷ 5 = 6 using a multiplication fact instead?"
  • "If we skip-counted by 5 exactly ten times, what's the biggest number we'd reach? How do you know?"
  • "Why does 5 × 5 mean five groups of five, and not just five plus five?"
Hint escalation ladder (use in this order — never give the answer first)
  1. Level 1 — Encourage: "Take your time, you've got this. What's the first thing you'd try?"
  2. Level 2 — Point to the pattern: "Look at the skip-counting track we made earlier — where does this number fit?"
  3. Level 3 — Model the first step: "Let's do the first jump together: 5... now you continue."
  4. Level 4 — Partial answer: Only if still stuck after Level 3, give the first two numbers of the sequence and ask them to finish it.
Case Study 5 — error-spotting question

When the student reaches "Which calculation is WRONG?" (answer: 5×5=20), pause after they submit and ask: "Can you fix it and tell me the right answer out loud?" — this is your error-analysis checkpoint.

🎯 Goal by 00:40: student explains their reasoning in full sentences for at least 2–3 cases without you supplying the answer. Deviation budget: ≤30 seconds.
Bridging line into Stage 5"You're doing such careful thinking — now it's your turn to try some on your own. I'll be right here if you need me."

5️⃣ Independent Practice

Static Duration
00:40–00:52
Personalization Trigger #5 of 5 — Support vs Challenge routing
⭐ Support path🌟 Challenge path
If the student needed Level 3–4 hints more than twice in Stage 4 → narrate the ⭐ Support items slowly and offer to do the first Challenge item together instead of solo. If the student breezed through Stage 4 → let them attempt every 🌟 Challenge item independently first, only stepping in if they ask.
Check-in script — use at least 4 times during this stage
Say at roughly 42, 45, 48, and 50 minutes "How's it going? Can you show me your thinking on this one?" — then wait silently for them to explain before responding. Do not interrupt their thinking process.
Feedback phrase bank — specific, on the process, not just "correct!"
  • "I love how you skip-counted so carefully there!"
  • "Nice job catching that on your own — that's exactly what good mathematicians do!"
  • "You explained your thinking so clearly, I could follow every step."
  • "That was a tricky one and you didn't give up — great effort!"
  • "I noticed you double-checked your answer — that's a smart habit."
  • "You used the pattern we found earlier — perfect connection!"
  • "It's okay that one was wrong — what can we learn from it?"
  • "You're really building speed with your 5 times table today!"
🎯 Goal by 00:52: student completes the ⭐ Support items independently, and attempts most 🌟 Challenge items with at most light support. Deviation budget: ≤30 seconds.
Bridging line into Stage 6"You worked so hard today — let's take a moment to look back at everything you learned."

6️⃣ Reflection, Closing & Homework

Static Duration
00:52–01:00
Two-way reflection dialogue (≥3 questions — read the student's typed answer out loud, then respond)
  1. After they type their answer to Q1 ("one new thing you learned"), respond with a specific follow-up: "Tell me more — how would you explain that to your little brother/sister/friend?"
  2. After Q2 ("explain in your own words how to multiply by 5"), listen for the words "skip count" or "add 5 each time" — if missing, gently prompt: "What did we do with our fingers earlier?"
  3. After Q3 (confidence scale 1–5), respond warmly regardless of the number: if low, reassure and note it for next session's warm-up; if high, celebrate it.
Closing script — say word for word "You did an amazing job today — you went from reviewing the 2 times table all the way to solving tricky word problems with the 5 times table! I'm really proud of how you [specific thing they did well, e.g., caught your own mistake / explained your thinking]. Keep practicing with your homework, and next time we'll build on this even further. See you next class!"
Follow-up practice

Assign the 10 homework problems shown in the Lesson Material (student sees them on screen — no need to re-read all 10 aloud, just confirm they can see the list and know to write answers in their notebook). Mention next lesson will likely move to the 10 times table or mixed review, to give a sense of progression.

🎯 Goal by 01:00: reflection captured, homework assigned and understood, class ends on a warm, encouraging note, 0 minutes over time.
